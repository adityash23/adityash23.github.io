Breaking my usual routine of waking up straight for my 10 am classes, 8th of November had an early start to the day. With eyes barely open, I didn't have enough time to make breakfast and headed straight to a Canadian classic - Tim Hortons (you guessed it right!). Utilizing the offers available, I nourished myself to a breakfast sandwich and a (decaf) coffee.

It was the day of the [Northeast North America Regional Contest](https://ne.na.icpc.global), where universities send their brightest minds to compete in a regional stage with different universities across the Northeast North America region. At McGill, the selection for those representing our university was done based on the performance at the event NP Compete which serves as the [North America Qualifier](https://na.icpc.global/naq/) (NAQ). The Northeast Regionals were taking placing across multiple different host universities across Canada and USA and students competed from the nearest host university to them. Lucky (or maybe not cause I don't get to travel to some new city) for me, the site closest to me was my own, lovely McGill University at the classic Trottier Building.

As I reached the location hosting the competition, I saw a group of people, all energized and hyped up about the 5 hours long intense programming competition that awaited them. During registration, my team and I were asked to give our mobiles and electronic devices that will be safely stored away for the duration of the competition. I kinda knew that but still the thought of not using my phone for 5-6 hours was very strange especially with the over reliance and limited attention span thanks to short form content. 

*If I get bored solving questions, what do you mean I can't scroll reels for a quick rush of dopamine?*

The event was the day for a lot of firsts - first ICPC regionals, first time solving problems for 5 hours straight and the first time being so long without using my phone! A key takeaway is a bblessing that if I find something engaging enough (and something that keeps me fed throughout the duration), I actually *can* stay without my phone. Thank you ICPC!
