Following are some of my key takeaways from the 2026 (Business Ethics Conference on Ethics and Artificial Intelligence)[https://www.mcgill.ca/desautels/channels/event/lcbee-business-ethics-conference-ethics-and-artificial-intelligence-373737] organized by the  (Laidley Centre for Business Ethics and Equity)[https://www.mcgill.ca/desautels/initiatives-institutes/laidley-centre-business-ethics-and-equity-lcbee].

- As advancements in Artificial Intelligence happen at ever so increasing pace, organizations must place an emphasis on clean energy sources powering data centers instead of just relying on using energy from the traditional grid powered by fossil fuels. The latter not only has a significant carbon footprint but also affects the access to energy for those living in the regions surrounding the data centers (due to higher electricity costs, erratic electricity supply etc).

- As we see AI capabilities advance - going from simple textual, next-word prediction to deeper reasoning tasks to voice and video generation - we also see a proportional increase in the compute and energy resources needed to power such advanced capabilties. Thus, we must decide which use cases are worth the resources. For example, in the context of video generation, we should decide if it is better to generate meme videos or helpful, instructional and explanatory content (albeit one might be funnier than the other).

- The AI wave started with heavily subsidized token prices to encourage its usage and widespread adoption. As companies start to explore finally gaining profits, the token costs are starting to rise. This rise in prices raises the question of ethical usage - how do we ensure that researchers (especially those with limited funding resources) are still able to access and use these models to solve real world problems and the model usage is not limited to just big corporations with a vast amount of money.

- The AI lifecycle encompasses multiple processes - from sourcing Helium, Silicon Oxide and chip assembling machines to sourcing data and energy. These processes have their own raw materials, coming from all over the world. When countries talk about sovereignty and independence in the AI race, we should choose and materialize that the independence comes from which part of this process. It would be a challenging task to get full autonomy across all aspects of it so it's better to specialize in a particular process and making ourselves resilient in it.
